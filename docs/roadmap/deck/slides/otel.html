403 Domain model track: OTEL 2.0

The model

  • Gemma 4 31B-IT, post-trained (OSFT) on about 440B telecom tokens: 3GPP, ETSI, ITU, GSMA, CAMARA, O-RAN, TM Forum, plus the Telco Corpus
  • Apache-2.0; vLLM 0.19+ in bf16; TP=1 on 192 to 288 GB cards, TP=2 on 80 GB
  • Top of the Open Telco AI leaderboard; released July 24, 2026

What its card says

  • The training mix has no telecom-specific tool-calling, MCP or instruction-following data
  • Not benchmarked for closed-loop network tasks; tool readiness unproven
  • Recommends retrieval, source attribution and human review for high-impact use

What 403 measures, then tunes

  • Tool-calling on the 101 and 201 tapes, with wire logs as evidence
  • Judge groundedness with the 302 suite; closed-loop behavior in 301 (co-decision, gate, rollback)
  • Cost per resolved episode as the small tier in 201 routing and course 203
  • Then SDG Hub turns the 401 catalogs and the tapes into the telecom tool-calling and MCP data the card lacks, and OSFT folds it in without forgetting

SERVING HOME OPTIONS

AMD MI300X or MI355X at TP=1 · Azure GPU · Venice’s 96 GB card at TP=1 with a short context · a MaaS endpoint. The courses swap models by config: the endpoint contract stays the same.

OTEL 2.0 drops into the model slot every course already treats as a stateless endpoint. The model card is candid about what it does not cover, and those three gaps are exactly what the courses measure: tool-calling, groundedness, and closed-loop behavior. The card credits Red Hat for OSFT and SDG Hub, Microsoft Azure for data processing, AMD for MI300X and MI355X compute, and GSMA for the corpus.
